* I am a PhD researcher at the [Spoken Language Systems (LSV)](https://www.lsv.uni-saarland.de/) group, Saarland University, where I work on applying language models to chemistry. I am advised by [Prof. Dr. Dietrich Klakow](https://www.lsv.uni-saarland.de/people/dietrich-klakow/).
* Before this, I worked on computational musicology and generative models for music with latent diffusion models at the University of Galway. Further back, I spent several years in industry as a software engineer at companies like Salesforce and Delhivery, before making a deliberate move toward research.
* I received my Master's in Informatics (Artificial Intelligence) from Università della Svizzera italiana (USI), Lugano, and my Bachelor's in Computer Science & Engineering from IIT Hyderabad.
* Outside academia, I am a guitarist and movie enthusiast. I am broadly curious about the cognitive and computational foundations of intelligence—whether minds are truly something new that emerges from scale and data, or whether something essential is still missing.

Research interests
======

My work sits at the intersection of machine learning and chemistry. Right now, I am especially interested in how language models can help us understand molecules:

* Predicting molecular properties using chemical language models
* Testing what large language models really understand about chemistry
* Improving how these models reason about chemical problems
* Exploring in-context learning for chemistry tasks

More broadly, I am trying to understand the limits of this approach. How far can we push chemistry through language modeling? And where does it start to break completely?

If you are interested in this space—maybe as a thesis student or collaborator—feel free to reach out at pjajoria (at) lsv dot uni-saarland dot de.
